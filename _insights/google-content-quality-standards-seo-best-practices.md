<h1>Original or Outranked: How to Survive Google’s Content Quality Standards</h1>
It’s a fact that modern content marketing lives and dies on search engines, and that means catering to the ever-changing whims of companies like Google’s algorithms and content standards.
In today’s age, when the ubiquity of AI summaries has had a serious impact on the way internet users search for information (and by that same token, the visibility of online campaigns) understanding the various ins and outs of Google content guidelines is absolutely critical for any content marketer who wants to succeed in an increasingly competitive landscape. 
In this article, we’re going to break down what Google’s new content quality standards actually are, our SEO content best practices, and how you can use them to craft more compelling, effective content and reach a wider audience than ever before. Read on to learn more. 
<h2>What Are Google’s Rating Guidelines?</h2>
Google has updated its rules to include a new definition of artificial intelligence (AI). Generative AI is defined as “a type of machine learning (ML) model that can take what it has learnt from the examples it has been provided to create new content, such as text, images, music, and code.” The description adds that: “Generative AI can be a helpful tool for content creation, but like any tool, it can also be misused.”
Bearing this in mind, Google has stated that their lowest-rated content is now what’s called "spammy" search results. Namely:
"Reposts on social media with little further discussion or commentary."
"Pages containing content from other sources (such as pages with "repinned" images or embedded videos) with minimal extra commentary, discussion, or curation by the page's content creator."
"Derivative listicle articles with minimal original content or based on reviews that already exist from other sources."
In short, Google will rate any content that simply recycles from elsewhere extremely low, and will contrariwise rate highly any content that strives for originality, timeliness, relevancy, and expertise.
<h3>The EEAT Checklist</h3>
That said, content marketers would do well to bear Google’s new ‘EEAT’ checklist in mind. This acronym stands for::
Experience: The content provided should come from the first-hand experience of the author. It shouldn’t be something they’ve heard about from another source and have chosen to pass off as their own wisdom.
Expertise: The source should have some background knowledge about whatever subject they are writing about. If they don’t have that knowledge already, then they should have done a decent level of research and deep-diving into the topic to know what they’re talking about.
Authority: The content should stem from a source with authority, operating within a content network of other responsible and like-minded content providers who have the experience, knowledge, and evidence to back up their claims. However, if the brand, source, or marketer does not yet possess the right level of authority, then they should quote or cite sources that do or reach out to thought leaders for comment. 
Trustworthiness: The content should be reliable and fact-checked, and the brand that supports it should strive to be a reputable one. We’ve recently discussed this very topic in our white paper on the dangers posed by misinformation. In essence, this means that marketers have an obligation and a duty to ensure that their content is verifiable and does not mislead the public.
<h2>How to Improve the Quality of Your Content with Google’s Guidelines</h2>
So, with all this in mind, how can marketers abide by these guidelines and ensure their SEO content is highly rated?:
<h3>Embrace "People-First" Content</h3>
The first principle you should strive to keep in mind before writing any piece. Every piece of content you create should genuinely aim to help, inform, or entertain your target audience. By keeping your target audience personas in mind — not as a series of generic traits, but as real, emotional, thinking human beings — you’ll ensure that your work never loses its sense of humanity. You should try to understand why your audience is searching for the content you’re writing, and then break that issue down into the essential information they need. 
In addition, you should take care not to simply regurgitate information already available. Human beings are naturally attracted to surprise and novelty, so consider when crafting your content: “What is the special ‘X-factor’ I need to make this stand out among the Offer unique insights, fresh perspectives, original research, case studies, real-life examples, and actionable advice.
You also need to remember that the best human-written content knows what a good reading experience is. With this in mind, the better written your content is for readers, the better it will invariably perform. Ensure your prose is concise, easy to read, and conversational in tone, and ensure that there is a clear personal perspective if possible.
Finally, avoid lazy practices that focus on article performance and not readability and relevance, such as keyword stuffing or flooding your channels with high volumes of low-value content via automation.
<h3>Double Down on EEAT</h3>
Now, let’s consider each of Google’s guidelines, and discuss how to best engage with them for better SEO results.
Experience: Firstly, as an author or publisher your brand should always strive to demonstrate real-life familiarity with the topic. This could mean writing anecdotally. Obviously, you shouldn’t present your lived experience as universal and objective. But you shou;n’t be afraid to share personal stories to show you have hands-on experience of a given subject, however tangential. 
And if you don’t have personal experience of your subject matter already? Well, seek it out! Attempting to engage with that personal experience can form the backbone of a piece of content — giving you the ideal autobiographical structure to begin writing about it. 
Expertise: All your content should attempt to establish some degree of thought leadership, so don’t be shy about showing off your knowledge and proficiency in your chosen field. This is your opportunity to boast your own credentials and show how much you know. 
But if you lack the requisite field in a topic, then don’t panic! You can always take the journalistic approach and seek sources of expertise from other people or organizations. Seek out other subject matter experts for quotes and insights, and include author bios with credentials, background, and relevant experience. The more legitimate they are (and the fresher their commentary is) the better Google will rank your content. 
Finally, ensure you should demonstrate your knowledge by ensuring that you have dealt with your chosen subject in enough depth. Don’t skim over the details or water down your information. Explore all the relevant subtopics and common pain points you can, and don’t be afraid to employ appropriate industry-specific language and concepts when you can.
Authoritativeness: You should also try to better establish your brand's reputation as a go-to source by drawing upon information from sources who are thought of as authorities. By associating with them, you’ll be counting your own brand as their equal. So, try to cite credible sources and link to authoritative websites in your field. Strive to create content that naturally earns mentions and links from other respected sites.
Trustworthiness: Finally, don’t forget about the prime importance of trust; an all-too human emotion. You can spew as much useful information as you like, but if your chosen audience (or Google for that matter) doesn’t regard you as credible, you’ll have a hard time ranking highly in any search results. 
So, ensure that your content conveys a sense of trustworthiness, integrity, and accuracy. As we’ve discussed in our ebook, try to be as transparent as you can about your research and fact-checking (which should be diligent and vigorous) and how your content was created. In addition, strive to provide accessible privacy policies, terms and conditions, and contact information, and own up to any mistakes you might make if you do end up dealing with potential misinformation.
<h3>Optimize for Page Experience</h3>
Finally, don’t forget to provide a good technical experience on whatever page or channel you choose to publish your content on. Again, this will provide a clear signal to Google that your content has been made with effort and care. Ensure that your website and content is easily accessible on all devices (mobile, tablet, desktop), as  Google uses mobile-first indexing, and will rate pages that are more accessible higher. And don’t forget to include clear and detailed metadata as well.
In terms of the content itself, ensure higher levels of readability through clear headings, subheadings, short paragraphs, bullet points, and appropriate font sizes. This will make your content easy to scan and digest, and mean that Google will rate it highly. Ensure you incorporate high-quality visuals, such as (relevant) images, videos, and infographics that enhance understanding and engagement. Optimize them for fast loading with descriptive alt text.
By focusing on these principles – creating genuinely helpful, expert-driven content for your audience, optimizing for a positive page experience, and regularly auditing your content – you'll be well-positioned to survive and thrive under Google's evolving content quality standards.
